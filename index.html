Our Research

Engineering programmable proteins to dissect and control disease-related signaling pathways.

🧬

De novo Protein Therapeutics

We design compact, stable protein binders, agonists, and antagonists for disease-relevant receptors — including insulin receptor, BMP receptors, and integrins — using AI-guided computational methods such as RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN.

⚡

Programmable Signaling Control

We create synthetic ligands and molecular traps that tune pathway specificity, potency, and timing — enabling precise intervention in metabolic disease, cancer, and regenerative medicine.

🔬

Synthetic Cell Engineering

We build cells with synthetic receptors, novel ligands, and rewired signaling pathways. Our orthogonal ligand–receptor systems precisely control cellular behavior in therapeutic contexts.

🖥️

High-throughput Design-to-Function

Iterative design–build–test cycles link AI-based design tools with yeast/mammalian display, SPR, ITC, and cryo-EM to rapidly evolve functional proteins.

🏗️

Structural Biology

Cryo-EM, X-ray crystallography (15+ PDB depositions), and NMR provide atomic-level understanding of how our designed proteins engage their targets and guide the next design iteration.

💊

Biologics & Drug Discovery

Located in Northeastern's Center for Drug Discovery, we translate designed proteins toward therapeutic leads targeting insulin/IGF-1 receptor, BMP, and integrin α5β1 pathways.

Xinru Wang, Ph.D.
Principal Investigator · Assistant Professor
Center for Drug Discovery, Pharmaceutical Sciences · Bouvé College of Health Sciences · Northeastern University, Boston MA
Dr. Wang designs programmable protein therapeutics to control cell signaling. She pioneered de novo protein regulators of disease-related signaling pathways during postdoctoral training with Dr. David Baker at the University of Washington (2020–2026). She earned her Ph.D. in Molecular Biology, Biochemistry & Cell Biology from Brown University (advisors: Drs. Rebecca Page & Wolfgang Peti) and her B.S. in Biochemistry from the University at Albany. She is a 2023 Leading Edge Fellow and co-founder of Lila Biologics.
